ABSTRACT  Skin cancer is one of the most common types of cancer globally. Despite the remarkable 
advancements of deep learning methods in computer vision, automatic diagnosis of skin diseases still faces 
challenges such as limited data and class imbalance. Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), which can 
synthesi ze realistic data, ap pear as an alternative to mitigate these issues.  However, for imbalanced data, 
unconditional GANs either generate uneven data distribution or neglect universal knowledge of the whole 
dataset, while state-of-the-art (SOTA) conditional GANs suffer from performance degradation due to the 
mode collapse of minority classes.  This paper proposes a two -stage GAN -based method to synthesize fine -
grained and diverse 256× 256 pixels skin lesion images for the imbalanced dataset, named Self -Transfer 
GAN (STGAN). STGAN first learns universal knowledge from all classes then transfer s this shared 
knowledge to each class and fuse s it with class -specific knowledge to synthesize high -quality images. 
Further more, based on STGAN, a framework to enhance the classification performance is established. Both 
data generation and classification tasks are evaluated on HAM10000 dataset. In terms of Fré chet Inception 
Distance (FID), Inception Score, Precision , and Recall, STGAN improved by 16%, 16%, 4%, 33% 
compared with SOTA conditional StyleGAN2. For classification, the STGAN -based framework achieves 
remarkable results, with an Accuracy of 98.23%, Sensitivity of 88.85%, Precision of 90.23%, F1 -score of 
89.48%, and Specificity of 98.34%.

I. INTRODUCTION  
Skin cancer  has become one of the most prevalent types of 
cancer worldwide, with approximately 9500 cases in the 
United States every day [1]. It encompasses various types, 
including basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, 
melanoma, etc.  [2]. Among these, melanoma is the most 
dangerous and accounts for the highest number of death s 
[3]. However, studies have demonstrated that skin cancer 
can be effectively treated when diagnosed at early stage 
and managed appropriately [3], [4] . Therefore, early 
detection is crucial for improving the survival rate of skin 
cancer patients.   
The most important aspect of the clinical diagnosis and 
treatment of skin cancer lines in the observation of skin 
lesions. Dermoscopy, a non -invasive imaging technique, 
provides rich details and accurate color representation of skin lesions, making it widely applied in the diagnosis of skin 
diseases. With the use of dermoscopy imaging, the diagnostic 
accuracy can be boosted to 75% -84% [5]. However, the 
visual examination of dermoscopy images by dermatologists 
is commonly perceived as a challenging and time -consuming 
task, often subject to errors and subjectivity [6]. Moreover, 
for patients residing in remote areas, it is hard to access 
specialized dermatologists. Hence, an automated computer -
aided diagnostic system is highly demanded.  
Despite the significant advancements achieved by recent 
deep learning methods in visual data, they still face following 
challenges  in automated skin disease diagnosis. First, the 
available annotated data are severely limited, as image 
acquisition and labeling require specialized equipment and 
trained medical professionals. Taking the publicly available  This article has been accepted for publication in IEEE Access. This is the author's version which has not been fully edited and

dataset is 58.3.  The class with the largest sample size , 
melanocytic nevus (NV) , has 6705 images , while the class

(CNN ) classifier architectures  are typically designed to work 
well for balanced datasets, they tend to treat minority classes 
as rare occurrences, or even as noise, when confronted with 
inter-class imbalance  [11]. Intra-class imbalance refers to the 
attribute bias within a class , it can be considered as a form of 
inter-class imbalance in the context of fine -grained visual 
categorization. Intra-class imbalance can also negatively 
affect the performance of CNN classifiers . The presence of 
intra-class imbalance is closely intertwined with  the “small 
disjuncts problem ” [12], [13] , a problem that has been 
demonstrated to significantly impair  classification 
performance [14]. Small disjuncts  refers to classification 
rules that cover only a limited number of training samples , 
and the scarcity of samples renders rule induction more 
susceptible to errors  [15], [16] .  
Traditional Data Augmentation (TDA) techniques,  such as 
color  space transformations  (e.g., adjusting  saturation, 
contrast, brightness, and hue of the training image ), 
geometric transformations  (e.g., flipping, cropping, rotation, 
translation ), mixing images, and random erasing, can 
effectively inflate  the size of training dataset, thereby 
mitigat ing the issue s of limited labeled data and class 
imbalanc e problem [17]. Geometric and color space 
transformations are the most commonly employed data 
augmentation techniques in skin disease classification tasks 
[18]. Perez , et al.  [19] investigated 13 TDA scenarios for 
skin lesion classification trained on CNN  classifiers , their 
experiments revealed that color space transformations and 
geometric transformations could enhance the classification 
performance . However, image mixing and random erasing 
did not yield improvements, this is attributed to the fact that 
these two data augmentation techniques cannot guarantee 
that the transformed  images retain the relevant skin lesion  
features of the source images. The works in [20], [21] also 
reported that the application of geometric transformations 
and color space augmentations could improve the 
performance of skin lesion classification.  Although TDA  
techniques , like geometric and color space transformations, 
can improve skin lesion  classification performance, it faces 
some drawbacks. First, the transformed images with small 
modifications have a similar distribution to the original 
images  and do not bring any new visual features to the 
training data , resulting in limited improvement in 
performance  [22], [23] . Additionally, the associated 
distortion magnitude parameter of geometric and color space 
transformations need to be carefully chosen, since heavily 
transformed  images could alter the semantic meaning of the 
medical  images [17], [24] . Moreover, for imbalanced dataset, naï ve augmentive oversampling with basic image 
transformations could cause overfitting on the minority class 
[17]. 
In contrast to TDA,  Generative Adversarial Networks 
(GANs) [25] aim to  learn the underlying data distribution and 
generate realistic  looking images . Recent advancements in 
GANs indicate that their ability to represent complex and 
high-dimensional data can serve as an advanced data 
augmentation method  to increase the amount of training 
samples and mitigate the imbalance problem of skin lesion 
dataset  [22], [26], [27], [28], [29], [30] . Basically, there are 
two GAN -based solutions for data augmentation, one is 
unconditional GANs and the other is conditional GANs [31], 
[32], [33] . As for unconditional GANs, there are two training 
regime s. One regime  involves training a single unconditional 
GAN model using the entire training set. A drawback of this 
training regime  is that when the training data is imbalanced, 
the distribution of generated data between different classes 
remains imbalanced as well [34], leading to exacerbation of 
the class imbalance issue in downstream classification tasks; 
The other regime  is to train an unconditional GAN model for 
each class separately. However, this regime  fails to utilize 
global knowledge or information that may be available 
across all classes.  
On the contrary, conditional GANs incorporate class labels 
during the training process, enables it to explore the universal  
knowledge and class specific knowledge in one model, 
making it a natural choice for multi -class image generation. 
However, findings in [35] reveal that despite achieving 
impressive results on metrics, samples generated by 
conditional GANs lack diversity. Moreover, it has been 
recently found that, state-of-the-art ( SOTA ) conditional 
methods like BigGAN [36] and StyleGAN2 [37] with 
augmentation, suffer performance degradation due to the 
mode collapse of minority classes [38]. Furthermore, the 
performance of conditional GANs degrade more in 
comparison with unconditional GANs in imbalanced 
scenario [39]. 
For investigating the mode collapse phenomenon of 
conditional GANs on minority classes further, we train the 
recently SOTA conditional StyleGAN2 (cSG2) with 
Adaptive Discriminator Augmentation (ADA) [40] on

approximately 2.5k iterations. This suggests that the model

Based on the reasons discussed above,  a two -stage GAN -
based method for skin lesion image synthesis,  named 
STGAN, is proposed in this study. STGAN  decouples the 
learning of universal knowledge and class -specific 
knowledge to mitigate the mode collapse of minority classes. 
Specifically,  an unconditional GAN is first trained using all 
samples to learn the shared universal knowledge of skin 
lesion images, and then the learned  universal knowledge is 
propagated to each class and fuse d with class -specific 
knowledge to generate images for each class in second stage. 
Furthermore, given the very limited number of samples for 
some classes in the second stage,  self-supervised learning [41] and Freeze -D [42] are introduced in the  discriminator to 
enhance sample efficiency.  
In contrast to conditional GANs, STGAN builds upon the 
knowledge transfer and separates the learning of global and 
class -specific knowledge. This separation offers two 
advantages. First, the global knowledge acquired by the 
model in the first stage facilitates efficient learning of class -
specific knowledge. Second, this separation allows for better 
control over the learning of class -specific knowledge, thereby 
mitigating mode collapse of the minority classes.

enhance the skin lesion diagnosis performance for limited 
and imbalanced data is established (Fig. 3). First, STGAN is 
utilized to generate diverse and visual -appealing images, then 
the generated data and training data are combined to train a 
ResNet50 [43] model, which is initialized with pre -trained

II. RELATD WORK  
In this section, the review and discussion of previous 
research closely related to this study are conducted.  The 
related work s are categorized  into three main themes: 
imbalanced data, GAN transfer learning and GANs in skin 
lesion image synthesis.  
A. IMBALANCED DATA  
Data imbalance refers to a situation in a dataset where one or 
more classes have a much larger samples compared to other 
classes. Numerous efforts have been devoted to developing 
classification models for this uneven data distribution [13], 
[46], [47], [48] . However, only a limited number of studies 
have focused on learning generative models for imbalanced 
data distribution. Rangwani , et al.  [38] proposed a group 
Spectral Regularizer (gSR) for conditional GANs to alleviate 
mode collapse. Rangwani , et al.  [49] introduced continuity in 
class label embedding vectors to improve the quality of 
generated images for minority classes. However, these 
techniques are primarily designed for conditional GANs. 
Rangwani , et al.  [39] found that the performance of 
conditional GANs deteriorates more in the presence of data 
imbalance compared to unconditional GANs and proposed 
the use of a pre -trained classifier to guide an unconditional 
GAN in generating high -quality images for each class. 
Nonetheless, the prerequisites for a well -pretrained classifier 
in [39] could potentially impose limitations.  
B. GAN TRANSFER LEARNING  
Transfer learning is a machine learning technique that utilizes 
pre-trained parameters from other dataset as the initial 
parameters, rather than random initialization. By adopting this approach, knowledge can be transferred from one dataset 
(i.e., source domain) to another (i.e., target domain), resulting 
in reduced training time and data size requirements. Several 
researches have explored transfer learning in the context of 
GANs [42], [50], [51], [52] . Wang , et al.  [52] is one of the 
first to explore transfer learning in GANs. Their experimental 
findings revealed that leveraging knowledge from pre -trained 
models can lead to reduced training time and enhanced image 
generation quality in scenarios with limited data. Noguchi 
and Harada [50]  introduced a novel approach that involves 
keeping all convolutional kernel parameters fixed and only 
updating shift and scale parameters in the generator. Wang , 
et al.  [51] proposed a miner network to identify a narrower 
region of the prior distribution where images generated by 
pre-trained model are similar to the target data, so as to ease 
the following finetuing step on the target data. Mo, et al.  [42] 
proposed a simple method, Freeze -D, which fixs the lower 
layers’ parameters in discriminator when fine -tuning GANs.  
While GAN -based transfer learning techniques are 
effective in addressing the limited data problem, their effect 
on imbalanced dataset have not been well explored.  Different 
from GAN -based transfer learning, the proposed method 
STGAN address the problem of imbalanced learning in 
GANs. Moreover, despite both being built upon the concept 
of knowledge transfer, STGAN involves transferring shared 
knowledge between different classes to each individual class 
in one domain, whereas current GAN -based transfer learning 
focuses on transferring knowledge between different 
domains.  
C. GAN IN SKIN LESION IMAGE SYNTHESIS  
Yi, et al.  [30] utilized categorical generative adversarial 
network (catGAN) [53] with Wasserstein distance [54] for 
dermoscopy images feature learning. They generate 64× 64 
pixels images and achieve an average precision score of 
0.424 with only 140 labeled images from the International 
Skin Imaging Collaboration  (ISIC) 2016 Challenge dataset 
[55]. Shoohi and Saud [56]  proposed using unconditional 
DCGAN [57] to synthesis 64× 64 pixels samples for the 
minority class for imbalanced Malaria dataset. However, skin 
lesion images should be generated in a high -resolution (e.g., 
256× 256 pixels) as it enables the identification of 
malignancy markers that differentiate between benign and 
malignant lesions with higher accuracy .

images, Baur , et al.  [58] proposed an unconditional deeply

skin lesion image. The effectiveness of DDGAN was 
compared with two other popular GAN architectures, namely 
the DCGAN [57] and LAPGA [59]. The results showed that 
both the DDGAN and LAPGAN are capable of learning the 
distribution of the datasets and synthesizing realistic samples. 
However, in the case of imbalanced data, the sample s 
generated by DDGAN remains class imbalance as well,

B. KNOWLEDGE TRANSFER AND FUSION  
The discriminator and generator trained in the first stage 
serve as the initialization for the second stage, where they are 
further trained on data from each individual class to learn 
class -specific knowledge. However, the binary classifiication 
task in this stage could be too easy for the discriminator to 
gain substantial discriminative capacity and providing 
sufficient feedback to train the generator. Thus, a more 
challenging task is assigned to the discriminator by 
employing self -supervised learning in the second stage to 
prevent overfitting. Furthermore, Freeze -D is also adopted to 
enhance training stability and improve knowledge transfer 
performance.  
1) SELF -SUPERVISED LEARNING  
Recent researches [62], [63], [64]  in self -supervised learning 
have shown that maximizing the agreement of different views (where views refer to same image go through different 
data augmentations) can facilitate the learning of informative 
and rich semantic feature representations by model. Drawing 
inspiration from this insight, STGAN  integrate self -
supervised learning into the discriminator in second stage. 
The advantage of employ self -supervised learning is twofold. 
First, if the discriminator is able to learn semantically -rich 
features, it will also be better at distinguishing real data from 
generated data, thereby prompting the generator to produce 
high-quality images. Second, enforcing the discriminator to 
learn high -quality features elevates the difficulty of its 
learning task, thereby preventing the discriminator from 
memorizing data in very limited samples and mitigating the 
risk of overfitting. By applying self -supervised learning, 
STGAN  can effectively leverage the limited samples in 
underrepresented classes, making the most out of the 
available data and improving the performance  in handling 
imbalanced dataset.

FIGURE 6.  Architecture of CNN classifier. (a) ResNet50. (b) T -ResNet50 . 
 In this work, 50 -layer ResNet (Deep Residual Networks)  
[43] is chosen as a CNN classifier. ResNet first introduced 
residual connections in DNNs, which addresses issues such 
as gradient vanishing and gradient explosion encountered in 
DNNs. This enables the training of very deep neural 
networks without compromising performance and has 
demenstraed excellent performance on various computer 
vision tasks, including skin lesion classification [65], [66], 
[67], [68] . Instead of training ResNet50 from scratch, we 
utilize transfer learning and initialize ResNet50 with 
ImageNet pre -trained weights (T -ResNet50) . Additionally, 
the original single fully connected layer in ResNet50 is 
replaced with two fully connected layers.  The first fully 
connected layer maps the 2048 -dimensional output from the 
flatten layer to a 128 -dimensional vector, while the second 
fully connected layer maps this 128 -dimensional vector to 7 
logits corresponding to the 7 classes in the HAM10000 
dataset (see Fig. 6(b)). 
As shown in Fig. 3, the training set is used to train the 
STGAN, and then the synthetic samples generated by 
STGAN are combined with the training set to further train 
the T -ResNet50 model. In the testing phase, instead of 
presenting the trained T -ResNet50 with the origin testing 
image, TTA is employed to further boost the classification 
performance. Concretely, we repeatedly introduce the TDA 
augmented images to the model, and compute the average 
predictions for each corresponding image and utilize this 
aggregated prediction as the ultimate inference.

V. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK  
In this paper, a two -stage GAN -based data augmentation 
method is proposed to enhance the classification performance 
of imbalanced skin lesion dataset. The proposed method 
decouples the learning of universal knowledge and class -
specific knowledge to mitigate mode collapse of minority 
classes. Additionally, self -supervised learning is introduced 
to improve sample efficiency, and Freeze -D is applied to 
enhance stability during the learning of class -specific 
knowledge.  Based on the proposed GAN -based data 
augmentation method, a framework to enhance the 
classification performance is established. E xperimental 
results on HAM10000 dataset showing that the proposed 
method can generate diverse and visually -appealing images 
and can provide additional information to improve the 
classification results. Accordingly, this study  can aid in the 
development of automated skin disease diagnosis system and 
contribute to improving the survival rates of individuals with 
skin cancer. A limitation of this study  is that due to the 
presence of natural artifacts, such as hair and veins, in the original training images, images  generated by the proposed 
method  also retain these artifacts. These artifacts may 
occlude the skin lesions and potentially cause the degradation 
of the classification performance. As a future direction, we 
intend to explore the use of image inpainting techniques to 
remove these natural artifacts before training and further 
boost the performance of GAN -based data augmentation in 
CNN -based classification.
