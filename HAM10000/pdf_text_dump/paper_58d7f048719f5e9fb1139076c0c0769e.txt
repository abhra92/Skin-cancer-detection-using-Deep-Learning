Early skin disease detection significantly improves patient survival rates, yet limited ac cess to 
dermatological expertise creates an urgent need for automated diagnostic systems. In this paper, 
we develop a dual-branch deep learning framework that simultaneously performs skin lesion 
segmentation and classification from dermoscopic images. The proposed segmentation branch 
uses a modified EfficientNet-B7 encoder with Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP) for multi-scale 
feature extraction and transformer blocks for global context modeling. Attention gates and Squeeze-
and-Excitation blocks enhance feature selection and boundary precision. The classification branch 
fuses DenseNet-121 visual features with morphological characteristics extracted from predicted 
segmentation masks, creating a hybrid appearance-morphology analysis approach. The proposed 
framework achieved strong and consistent segmentation performance across five benchmark datasets. 
On HAM10000, the highest Dice score (0.9568) and IoU (0.9242) were recorded, with an ac curacy 
of 0.9708. PH2 achieved a Dice of 0.9250 and a sensitivity of 0.9734, while ISIC 2016 reached a Dice 
of 0.9298 and an IoU of 0.8811. For ISIC 2017 and ISIC 2018, Dice scores were 0.8972 and 0.9020, 
respectively. All datasets reported high specificity (> 0.93) and accuracy (> 0.95), confirming the 
model’s robustness and generalization capability. Our dual-branch framework achieves state-of-the-
art accuracy by effectively integrating visual appearance and structural morphological features for 
comprehensive skin lesion analysis. The consistent high performance across diverse datasets indicates 
strong potential for clinical deployment as a diagnostic support tool.

We propose an integrated framework combining lightweight segmentation and morphology-aware 
classification branches, enabling comprehensive lesion analysis through both visual appearance and structural 
characteristics. The segmentation branch employs a modified EfficientNet-B7 encoder enhanced with Atrous 
Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP) and transformer-based global context modeling, while the classification branch

different encoder levels using parallel convolutional kernels with varying receptive fields (3 × 3, 5 × 5, 7 × 7).

accurate lesion delineation and characterization.

for refined feature selection and boundary accuracy. The attention mechanisms enable selective amplification 
of relevant features while suppressing noise and irrelevant information, significantly improving segmentation 
precision. The dual-stream classification approach uniquely combines DenseNet-121 processing of RGB 
images with dedicated convolutional analysis of segmentation masks, capturing morphological characteristics 
including boundary irregularities, asymmetry patterns, and shape complexity that are critical for diagnostic 
accuracy. We conduct systematic ablation studies quantifying individual component contributions and establish 
new performance benchmarks datasets. The evaluation encompasses both quantitative metrics and qualitative 
clinical assessment to ensure practical applicability.

Related work
The field of automated skin lesion analysis has undergone a significant transformation from traditional machine 
learning techniques to more advanced deep learning models. This section provides a summary of current 
methods used for analyzing skin lesions, which can be grouped by their technical approaches, including 
traditional machine learning, U-Net-based models, attention mechanisms, transformer-based models, and 
multi-scale feature fusion techniques. This review establishes the theoretical foundation and identifies the 
concrete research gaps that the presented framework aims to address.
From traditional methods to deep learning revolution
Early research into computational skin lesion analysis focused mainly on extracting handcrafted features and on 
classical machine learning models. Such conventional approaches used Adaptive thresholding, Decision trees 
and Support vector machines for lesion detection and segmentation. Under the entropy pooling, complicated 
manual feature designing, and their insufficient learning capacity for sophisticated medical images, their 
application to real-world medical areas is limited.
A fundamental step toward deep learning was taken by Esteva and colleagues18, who concluded that both 
deep and compact convolutional neural networks can reach dermatologist-level classification in skin cancer 
using 129,450 clinical images. In so doing, the work set the stage for all subsequent work by proving (to a firm 
stand) that deep learning is ‘allowed’ and superior for dermatology.
Automatic computer-aided diagnostic methods for the analysis of skin lesions: in the past, computer-aided 
diagnostic systems for the analysis of skin lesions were often based on handcrafted feature extraction, for 
example, color, texture, and shape descriptors, along with machine learning classifiers. These approaches required 
preprocessing steps like lesion segmentation, border detection, and feature calculation before classification. 
Although these techniques produced interpretable results, they were also subject to the variability introduced by 
imaging conditions, lesion heterogeneity, and manual feature design19. Another research proposed an algorithm 
that predicts melanoma progression by analyzing peritumoral regions in the Normalized 2-D Cross-Correlation 
(NCC) domain, using a mathematical morphological approach for accurate peritumoral detection.
Similarity assessment between melanoma interiors and surrounding areas was carried out using SSIM, 
achieving promising results on the 7-Point and PH2 datasets27. Another work modeled skin lesions as biological 
systems, leveraging morpho-granulometric color cluster features alongside machine learning classifiers such 
as Random Forest and XGBoost. By integrating feature selection with neural networks, the study improved 
classification performance for nevus vs. melanoma, achieving notable accuracy on multiple public datasets28. 
A recent approach employed an enhanced ISIC super pixel segmentation algorithm combined with graph 
cut methods to extract lesion regions. Geometric and shape features were then classified using various ML 
algorithms and neural networks, outperforming conventional techniques on 7-Point, MED-NODE, and PAD-
UFES-20 datasets29.
These contributions illustrate the transition from feature-engineering-based methods, which relied heavily 
on manual design and traditional image processing, toward end-to-end deep learning frameworks capable of 
learning robust representations automatically. This evolution sets the foundation for modern architectures of 
deep learning framework.
U-Net and encoder-decoder architectures
The addition of the U-Net architecture by Ronneberger et al.29 represented a breakthrough in the field of 
biomedical image segmentation. The symmetric encoder-decoder architecture of the network with skip 
connections allows for the capture of hierarchical features while preserving spatial resolution through multi-
scale feature fusion. The encoder slowly creates simpler versions of the image while making it smaller, and 
the decoder improves the detailed segmentations by using information from different scales. Based on this 
architecture, a variety of adaptations have been proposed targeting skin lesion segmentation. Zhou et al.30 
Scientific Reports  |        (2025) 15:37823 3 | https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-21783-zwww.nature.com/scientificreports/
introduced UNet++, which aims to lessen the loss of important information when transferring features from 
the encoder to the decoder by using closely linked skip connections. It drastically improved feature propagation 
and showed competitive performance in various medical image segmentation tasks. The combination of residual 
learning with U-Net structures has proven to be highly effective. Alom et al.31 introduced R2U-Net, which 
adopted residual connections within recurrent convolutional layers to improve the feature learning procedure. 
The hybrid U-Net performed better than the regular U-Net versions in various medical image analysis tasks, 
such as examining skin lesions.
Attention mechanisms and feature enhancement
The integration of attention mechanisms into segmentation architectures represents a significant advancement 
in medical imaging. Oktay et al.32 introduced The Attention U-Net employs attention gates to filter out 
unimportant areas while retaining important body parts. This attention mechanism allows networks to focus 
on diagnostically significant structures, leading to increased segmentation accuracy. The latest developments 
showed the need for personalized attentional mechanisms during the analysis of dermoscopic skin lesions. He et 
al.33 introduced an attention-based dual-path feature that is flexible enough to incorporate spatial and channel 
attention mechanisms to extract boundaries more effectively. Their approach achieved superior performance 
against the training set of existing state-of-the-art methods on a wide range of benchmark data sets and 
established the state-of-the-art results in terms of pixel-level accuracy.
Lama et al.34 proposed the Efficient_Net based encoder with squeeze-and-excitation residual block for skin 
lesion segmentation. Their method attained competitive performance and maintained computational efficiency, 
fulfilling the essential need to apply it in scenarios with resource limitations, like clinical settings.
Transformer-based architectures in medical segmentation
The emergence of Vision Transformers (ViTs) has catalyzed significant advancements in medical image 
segmentation20,35. Transformers excel at capturing long-range dependencies through self-attention mechanisms, 
addressing fundamental limitations of purely convolutional approaches in modeling global contextual 
information. Chen et al.36 proposed TransUNet, the first Transformer-based medical image segmentation 
model, which combines CNN feature extraction with Transformer-based global context modeling. This hybrid 
approach leverages the inductive biases of CNNs for local feature extraction while employing Transformers 
for capturing global dependencies, resulting in improved segmentation performance. Cao et al.37 introduced 
Swin-UNet, a pure Transformer-based U-shaped architecture that replaces traditional convolutional operations 
with hierarchical Swin Transformer blocks. The shifted window mechanism enables efficient computation while 
maintaining the ability to model both local and global dependencies effectively.
Wang et al.38 proposed UCTransNet, incorporating channel cross fusion with Transformer (CCT) and 
channel-wise cross-attention (CCA) modules to enhance feature fusion between encoder and decoder stages. 
This approach demonstrated superior performance specifically on skin lesion segmentation tasks, highlighting 
the potential of Transformer architectures for dermatological applications.
Multi-scale feature fusion and advanced context modeling
Multi-scale feature fusion has become increasingly critical for handling the diverse morphological characteristics 
of skin lesions. Yuan and Cheng39 recently proposed a multiscale context fusion algorithm based on UNet that 
extracts rich contextual information by aggregating semantic features from different encoding stages through a 
sophisticated three-branch feature fusion module.
Advanced attention mechanisms were only developed for this specific application of skin lesion analysis. 
Hou et al. pose a smart segmentation framework based on a deformable attention Transformer U-Net, with 
bidirectional attention mechanisms, which achieved the best performances on standard datasets.
Classification methodologies and integrated frameworks
Recently, researchers have proposed several architectures that incorporate multi-feature streams to improve 
the accuracy of skin lesion classification. Himel et al.40, reported that Vision Transformers work well on both 
segmentation and classification systems for dermoscopy, where they show better performance on various 
diagnoses compared to the traditional CNN-based method. Smarter use of metadata and contextual information 
has enjoyed enormous success in improving the accuracy of classification. The results of the ISIC 2019 challenge 
indicated that the use of pathological data and patient information along with multiple images of the lesions can 
greatly improve the precision of skin cancer diagnosis, highlighting the importance of comprehensive analysis to 
diagnose skin cancer. Cheng et al.23 proposed improved MobileNet architectures with the fused spatial channel 
(SC) attention mechanism to achieve the classification of skin cancer from images, confirming the advantages of 
lightweight architectures and attention mechanisms for clinical use.
Although some significant progress was achieved in automatic skin lesion analysis, there are still several major 
research problems that have not been satisfactorily solved. Existing methods tend to handle segmentation and 
classification in two separate, decoupled modes, which can cause the features of each task to be underexploited. 
Furthermore, most of the current methods only concentrate on single-task optimization, which may ignore the 
advantages of joint optimization.
The integration of advanced attention mechanisms with multi-scale feature fusion in unified frameworks 
represents a promising direction for future research. Furthermore, developing lightweight architectures that 
maintain diagnostic accuracy while enabling real-time clinical deployment poses a critical challenge that 
requires continued investigation.

Critical analysis of state-of-the-art approaches
The comprehensive review of existing methodologies reveals distinct strengths and limitations across different 
architectural paradigms that directly inform the design decisions for our proposed framework.
Traditional machine learning and early deep learning methods
Traditional approaches utilizing handcrafted features and classical machine learning algorithms demonstrated 
notable interpretability and computational efficiency, with the pioneering work by Esteva et al.18 establishing the 
fundamental viability of deep learning for dermatological applications. Their achievement of dermatologist-level 
performance provided crucial proof-of-concept for automated skin cancer detection and laid the foundation for 
subsequent research developments.
However, these methods exhibited significant limitations in feature representation capacity and generalization 
across diverse imaging conditions. The dependence on handcrafted feature extraction required extensive domain 
expertise and proved insufficient for capturing the complex, multi-scale patterns inherent in dermoscopic 
images. Manual feature engineering created substantial bottlenecks in adapting to new lesion types or imaging 
protocols, while classical classifiers demonstrated poor scalability when confronted with the high-dimensional 
nature of medical imaging data.
U-Net and encoder-decoder architectures
The U-Net architecture41 and its variants demonstrated remarkable success in medical image segmentation 
through their symmetric encoder-decoder structure with skip connections. These architectures effectively 
captured hierarchical features while preserving spatial resolution, enabling precise boundary delineation 
essential for lesion analysis. Standard U-Net architectures exhibited limited capacity for modeling long-range 
dependencies due to their purely convolutional nature. The fixed receptive field sizes constrained their ability 
to capture multi-scale contextual information effectively. Additionally, these methods focused exclusively on 
segmentation tasks without integrating classification capabilities, requiring separate systems for comprehensive 
lesion analysis and missing opportunities for joint optimization benefits.
Attention mechanisms and feature enhancement
Attention-based approaches, particularly Attention U-Net32 and squeeze-and-excitation networks34, 
demonstrated significant improvements in feature selection and boundary accuracy. These mechanisms enabled 
selective amplification of diagnostically relevant features while suppressing noise and irrelevant information, 
leading to enhanced segmentation precision across diverse lesion presentations.
Most attention mechanisms operated at limited spatial scales and lacked the capacity to integrate global 
contextual information effectively. The computational overhead introduced by attention mechanisms often 
compromised real-time inference capabilities. Furthermore, attention weights frequently lacked interpretability, 
making it difficult to understand which features drove diagnostic decisions—a critical limitation for clinical 
deployment.
Transformer-based architectures
Vision Transformers and hybrid CNN-Transformer approaches (TransUNet36, UCTransNet38 excelled at 
capturing long-range dependencies through self-attention mechanisms. These architectures demonstrated 
superior performance in modeling global context and handling irregular lesion shapes, addressing fundamental 
limitations of purely convolutional approaches.
Transformer-based models required substantial computational resources and large training datasets to 
achieve optimal performance, limiting their applicability in resource-constrained clinical settings. The quadratic 
complexity of self-attention mechanisms posed scalability challenges for high-resolution medical images. 
Additionally, these models often struggled with fine-grained local feature extraction compared to convolutional 
counterparts, potentially missing subtle textural patterns crucial for accurate diagnosis.
Multi-scale feature fusion approaches
Multi-scale fusion techniques22,39 effectively addressed the diverse morphological characteristics of skin lesions 
by integrating information across different spatial resolutions. These approaches demonstrated improved 
robustness to lesion size variations and enhanced boundary delineation for complex morphological patterns.
Existing fusion strategies often employed simple concatenation or addition operations without sophisticated 
mechanisms to weight the importance of different scales. The lack of adaptive fusion mechanisms resulted in 
suboptimal integration of multi-scale information. Most approaches failed to consider the semantic relationships 
between features at different scales, leading to redundant or conflicting information that could degrade overall 
performance.
Classification methodologies and integrated frameworks
Recent classification approaches utilizing Vision Transformers40 and enhanced MobileNet architectures23 
achieved competitive accuracy while maintaining computational efficiency. The incorporation of metadata 
and contextual information demonstrated significant improvements in diagnostic precision, highlighting the 
importance of comprehensive analysis beyond pure visual features.
The majority of classification systems operated independently from segmentation tasks, missing 
opportunities for mutual benefit through shared feature representations. Most approaches relied exclusively on 
appearance-based features without incorporating morphological characteristics that are clinically significant 
for dermatological diagnosis. The lack of integrated frameworks resulted in suboptimal resource utilization and 
potentially conflicting predictions between the segmentation and classification components.

Research gaps and motivations for proposed framework
The critical analysis reveals several key limitations that current state-of-the-art methods fail to address 
comprehensively:
 1. Task Isolation: The predominant focus on either segmentation or classification in isolation prevents the ex -
ploitation of synergistic relationships between these complementary tasks.
 2. Limited Morphological Integration: Most existing approaches neglect the incorporation of structural and 
morphological features that are diagnostically crucial in dermatological assessment.
 3. Scalability Constraints: Many high-performing methods require substantial computational resources, limit -
ing their deployment in resource-constrained clinical environments.
 4. Insufficient Multi-Modal Fusion: Current multi-scale fusion strategies lack sophisticated mechanisms for 
optimal integration of information from different representation levels.
These identified limitations directly motivated the development of our dual-branch framework, which integrates 
advanced segmentation capabilities with morphology-aware classification through innovative architectural 
designs that address the aforementioned shortcomings while maintaining computational efficiency for practical 
clinical deployment.

Algorithm 1  Proposed Framework for Automated Skin Lesion Segmentation and Classification
Segmentation architecture
In this work, we present a deep learning-based algorithm designed for automatic skin lesion segmentation, with 
an EfficientNet-B7-based encoder and a multi-scale attention-refined decoder. The framework focuses on precise 
segmentation and utilizes a densely computational effective feature extractor along with intricate attention and 
Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP) blocks. Unlike prior architectures, which only use RGB images as input, 
the encoder model in the proposed approach inputs a four-channel input, including the three RGB channels and 
an additional fractal dimension map. This fractal map is computed at inference time on-the-fly from the same 
dermoscopic image using the fractal analysis learned from the training data without data leak. Its application 
Scientific Reports  |        (2025) 15:37823 6 | https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-21783-zwww.nature.com/scientificreports/
provides texture information in addition to color or intensity information for more accurately showing the edge 
of lesions and small morphological structures. The fractal map is generated directly from the input dermoscopic 
image during training and inference; thus, it does not use any ground truth masks and prevents information 
leakage. The computation is performed as follows:
• Preprocessing: The RGB image is converted into a grayscale intensity representation to preserve structural 
details without color bias.
• Fractal Dimension Calculation: The box-counting method and optimized fractal descriptors are applied to 
quantify the spatial complexity of lesion regions. The fractal dimension is estimated across multiple scales to 
capture both fine and coarse structural variations.
• Normalization: The computed fractal map is normalized to the range [0, 1] to ensure consistency with other 
input channels and maintain stable training dynamics.
• Channel Integration: The normalized fractal map is concatenated with the original RGB channels, forming a 
four-channel input tensor fed into the segmentation encoder.
The proposed network is based on modified EfficientNet-B7 (M-EfficientNet-B7) as the backbone pretrained on 
ImageNet for feature extraction at multiple scales. Encoder features are processed with the stepic ASPP module 
and the transformer blocks for better modeling of global context and mounted via multi-scale fusion stage with 
skip connections. To enhance the quality of the segmentation, while maintaining the generalizability of the 
resultant model, the decoder has integrated with Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) and Attention Gates (AG) blocks 
which highlights the diagnostic area and suppresses the background.
At last, a 1 × 1 convolution layer with a sigmoid is used to yield a binary probability map where the high-
precision lesion regions are measured. This four-channel input strategy and the attention-based multi-scale 
architecture effectively alleviate skin lesion segmentation difficulties like irregular boundaries, diverse lesion 
sizes, and heterogeneous textures. Figure  1: The framework overview, RGB + fractal input fusion, and attention-
enhanced decoding.
Encoder architecture
The encoder in our model is based on M-EfficientNet-B4, a powerful convolutional neural network architecture 
pretrained on the ImageNet dataset. EfficientNet-B7 was chosen due to its superior accuracy and efficiency 
via scaling all dimensions of network width, depth and resolution using the compound scaling method, which 
balances the trade-off between accuracy and efficiency42. This scaling approach is formalized by Eq. 2:
 
 d=a∅,w=β∅,r=γ∅ (1)
where d represents depth, w denotes width, r indicates resolution, and α, β, γ are constants determined through 
grid search optimization.
EffectiveNet-B4 architecture is based upon MBConv layers, which are using depth-wise separable 
convolutions for computational efficiency in feature extraction. Every MBConv block includes an SE module, 
which adaptively recalibrates channel-wise feature responses and allows the network to devote more capacity 
to informative features. A residual skip connection is injected in each MBConv block for stable training and a 
reduction of the gradient vanishing. The proposed lesion segmentation framework employs M-EfficientNet-B7 
as a primary encoder, which learns hierarchical features from the four-channel dermoscopic input. The transition 
to four-dimensional input is performed by extending the initial convolutional layer with the extra channel. The 
encoder is composed of a series of MBConv blocks, which are designed to capture the feature complexity in the 
feature space, where shallow blocks are sensitive to low-level patterns (edges, textures) and deep blocks encode 
more abstract concepts of increasing complexity that are crucial for discriminating lesion boundaries.
The hierarchical feature extraction follows a structured progression:
• Initial Processing: The input volume is processed by the first two convolution blocks, generating low-level 
descriptors including edges, textures, and simple shapes.
• Mid-level Features: MBConv6 4×, k = 3 × 3 concentrates on moderately complex features that serve as foun -
dations for higher-level representations.
• High-level Abstractions: MBConv6 4×, k = 5 × 5 captures abstract representations vital for segmentation tasks.
• Complex Structures: MBConv6 6×, k = 3 × 3 refines features to capture increasingly complex image structures.
• Semantic Information: MBConv6 8×, k = 5 × 5, the deepest encoder layer, focuses on the most detailed and 
complex representations of the input image.
Atrous spatial pyramid pooling (ASPP) module
The Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP) module plays a central role in modern deep learning-based 
segmentation by expanding the receptive field and capturing multi-scale context without spatial down-sampling. 
Originally introduced in DeepLab v243, ASPP exploits dilated convolutions across multiple scales, preserving 
both semantic and spatial information through parallel Atrous convolutions with varying dilation rates rather 
than pooling operations.
The proposed architecture integrates ASPP downstream of the encoder to enhance the model’s capacity for 
recognizing and delineating small lesion regions. As illustrated in Fig.  2, the ASPP block comprises five parallel 
branches:
Scientific Reports  |        (2025) 15:37823 7 | https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-21783-zwww.nature.com/scientificreports/
 1. Four Atrous convolutions with dilation rates of 1, 6, 12, and 18 to capture contextual features at multiple 
scales.
 2. One image-level pooling branch to incorporate global context and mitigate filter degeneration.
The outputs of these five pathways are concatenated and fed into the subsequent transformer block, ensuring 
that the segmentation head has access to rich, multi-scale feature representations without spatial resolution loss.
Transformer module
The Transformer module is strategically positioned immediately after the ASPP module to enhance multiscale 
features with global contextual information. While ASPP effectively captures multi-scale contextual information 
through parallel Atrous convolutions, it inherently lacks the capacity to model long-range dependencies due 
to the localized nature of convolutional operations. The Transformer integration addresses this limitation by 
employing multi-head self-attention mechanisms that compute pairwise affinities across all spatial positions.

Transformer components
The Transformer module comprises several key components that contribute to enhanced feature representations:
• Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA): Enables each position in the input feature map to attend to all other 
positions, facilitating global context integration.
• Layer Normalization: Applied after MHSA and FFN sub-layers to stabilize learning by normalizing inputs 
across features, ensuring consistent gradient flow.
• Feed-Forward Neural Networks (FFN): Consist of two linear transformations with non-linear activation, 
processing each position independently to capture complex patterns.
• Residual Connections: Employed around both MHSA and FFN sub-layers to mitigate vanishing gradient 
issues and enable identity mapping learning.
The placement between ASPP and decoder is crucial for effective feature fusion. ASPP output, containing rich 
multi-scale contextual cues, is reshaped into token sequences suitable for Transformer processing. These tokens 
undergo successive Transformer encoder layers to produce globally contextualized feature representations that 
retain multi-scale details while incorporating image-wide dependencies.
Multi-scale fusion module (MSF)
The Multi-Scale Fusion Module is strategically positioned at the encoder’s end to perform concatenation and 
fusion operations on various feature maps ( f3, f4 from intermediate encoder stages and transformer block output) 
at different scales, generating comprehensive feature representations that better capture diverse features and 
boundaries.
The MSF incorporates multi-scale convolutional layers with kernel sizes of 3 × 3, 5 × 5, and 7 × 7, enhancing 
the model’s ability to capture features at varying spatial resolutions. This architecture allows successful extraction 
of fine-granular details as well as larger, more contextual features, which is important for skin lesion segmentation 
due to substantial size and appearance variations in the lesions.

from coarse ones via progressive upsampling, skip connections, and attention. The architecture is equipped with 
Attention Gates (AG) and Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) blocks for better feature refinement and selection.

Progressive reconstruction
All of the upsampling blocks (UP1–UP4) take features from the previous stage of decoding as well as from the 
associated levels of the encoder, which are then refined with an attention gate and recalibrated by a SE module. 
Such design allows insightful fusion of large-grained semantics with small-structured spatial hints to better 
delineate lesion boundaries and detect small structures.
The decoder produces a segmentation mask and the corresponding RGB image registered on the original 
input. The two network outputs are then sent to the classification calculation module for discrimination of them 
into one of the seven predefined skin disease categories, achieving accurate segmentation and providing dual-
purpose output for accurate classification.
Dual-branch classification architecture
The classifier model in our framework is a dual structure whose branches are designed well with visual look and 
structure information and can classify the skin lesion precisely. The network consists of three parts of architecture: 
the RGB Image Branch, the Mask Branch, and the Fusion and Classification Module. This multi-modal approach 
enables us to learn rich perception features from the raw dermoscopy images and the segmentation masks of 
these images and thus achieves a strong classification performance over the seven diagnostic classes of the 
HAM10000 data.
RGB image branch: denseNet-121 encoder
The RGB Image Branch uses DenseNet-12144, a strong and rapid convolutional neural network that is specifically 
designed for classifying images. DenseNet-121 represents a variant within the DenseNet family, comprising 121 
layers and approximately 8 million parameters. The architecture processes standardized 224 × 224 RGB input 
images through a series of densely connected blocks, where each layer receives feature maps from all preceding 
layers via concatenation operations.
This densely connected pattern provides several architectural advantages that are particularly beneficial 
for medical image analysis. The dense connectivity enhances gradient flow throughout the network, promotes 
extensive feature reuse across layers, and minimizes representational redundancy, collectively addressing the 
vanishing gradient problem inherent in deep neural networks. Between consecutive dense blocks, transition 
layers employ batch normalization, 1 × 1 convolutions, and 2 × 2 average pooling operations to down-sample 
feature maps while reducing computational complexity and parameter count. For integration into the proposed 
classification framework, the original classification head of DenseNet-121 has been modified to accommodate 
the specific requirements of skin lesion analysis, as illustrated in Fig.  2.

Notably, the dense connectivity structure not only leverages the learning capability but also retains feature 
propagation richness over different levels without any significant information loss from the network hierarchy, 
which can contribute to boosting the classification accuracy. A Global Average Pooling (GAP) layer is added 
before the output classification to reduce risks of overfitting and reduce the number of parameters with preserved 
useful spatial information.
The rate hyperparameter of a network, which determines increments of new feature maps provided by each 
layer, governs information flow and feature complexity. This architectural pattern has achieved state-of-the-
art results across several computer vision benchmarks and is thus a strong candidate to be incorporated into 
medical imaging applications that demand both high accuracy and computational efficiency.
Mask branch: morphological feature extraction
The Mask Branch is a vital component of the dual-branch architecture to capture the morphology and 
position information of the lesion in segmentation masks. In contrast to the branch that is employed to learn 
the discriminative visual features from the raw DIs (i.e., the RGB image branch), the mask branch provides 
complementary geometric information based on the shape and boundary properties of the lesion as well as the 
distribution and spatial properties (which are diagnostic in many dermoscopic examinations).
The mask branch design follows a cascaded conv structure, with each conv layer followed by batch norm and 
ReLU activations. It sequentially detects the hierarchies of spatial features from the binary input masks and can 
still reserve multiple morphological patterns at different scales. Then, the max-pooling operations are sampled 
such that the model is able to be locality aware on the informative pointers and is able to capture the spatial 
structure patterns, such as the irregular shapes and the asymmetrical arbitrary sizes of lesions—more obvious 
morphology signs of the cancers.
Based on the stack of convolutional stages with increasing receptive sizes, the network is expected to capture 
fine-grain boundary details and, simultaneously, the global shape information. After the convolutional feature 
extraction, the spatial features are aggregated into a single compact feature vector via global average pooling. The 
compact shape representation stores essential mask-dependent morphological parameters such as smoothness 
of the boundary, regularity of the shape, and the size of the shape. It has been shown that the feature vector is 
a global rich morphological feature representation, and it encodes structural characteristics that might not be 
observed in RGB space. Findings such as normal vessel-derived features like this might well be particularly 
relevant, especially when various types of lesion have look-alikes with anatomic morphologies.
Fusion and classification module
The Classify and Fusion Module combines features from both the RGB Image Branch and the Mask Branch to 
determine the final lesion category. At the fusion stage, the features of DenseNet-121 and the morphological 
features of the Mask Branch are concatenated and generate the unified feature vector to learn a complete pattern 
that contains the visual appearance and structure information.
After concatenating the three features, the fused representation is passed through a dense layer with ReLU 
activations and dropout regularization. The dropout, with properly tuned probability rates, regularizes the CNN 
model by avoiding overfitting and maintaining the generalization ability for various tumor presentations. The last 
output layer uses a softmax activation function for probability distributions over the seven HAM10000 categories: 
melanoma, melanocytic nevus, basal cell carcinoma, actinic keratosis, benign keratosis, dermatofibroma, and 
vascular lesion.
This cross-modal fusion system was designed to address the challenging task of dermatological diagnosis, 
which is problematic due to visual similarity between various lesion types, which results in misclassification 
using appearance-based features only. With the morphological priors from segmentation masks, the classification 
module is able to discriminate lesions with subtle appearance differences but obvious structure differences.
Combining the segmentation-based spatial priors with appearance-based visual features, a clued-up 
multimodality learning framework is established to improve disease classification accuracy significantly, 
generalization across different patient cohorts, and diagnostic reliabilities. This holistic fusion exploits the 
complementary characteristics of visual and morphological data and yields a higher performance than with 
unimodal approaches for skin lesion classification.

Implementation details
We implemented the dual-branch architecture using the PyTorch backend and executed all experiments on an 
NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU with 24 GB VRAM. For the segmentation task, all dermoscopic images were resized 
to 256 × 256 pixels, ensuring faster computation while preserving diagnostically important details. For the 
classification branch, images were resized to 224 × 224 pixels, the standard input size for DenseNet architectures, 
enabling effective utilization of pretrained weights. The segmentation encoder uses a four-channel input, which 
includes the standard RGB dermoscopic image plus an additional fractal dimension map as the fourth channel. 
This fractal map is computed directly from the input image during both training and inference, ensuring no 
dependency on ground truth masks and thereby preventing information leakage. The fractal map captures 
structural complexity and textural irregularities in the lesion region, complementing the color information 
provided by the RGB channels.
The network training setup included the AdamW optimizer for segmentation with a boundary loss function, 
chosen for its effectiveness in handling irregular lesion boundaries. For classification, the Adam optimizer with 
categorical cross-entropy loss was employed to address the multi-class nature of the problem. Training was 
performed with a batch size of 16, balancing GPU memory constraints and gradient stability. We trained for a 
maximum of 70 epochs for segmentation and 50 epochs for classification.

Evaluation metrics
The performance evaluation of the proposed dual-branch structure includes both segmentation and classification 
tasks and is evaluated with popular and widely used metrics in the field of medical image analysis and 
dermatological AI. This multi-metric evaluation paradigm enables a strong evaluation of the model’s capabilities 
in various aspects of skin lesion analysis.
Segmentation performance metrics
Six criteria were adopted for skin lesion segmentation evaluation: the Pixel Accuracy (PACC), the Specificity 
(SPE), the Sensitivity (SEN), the Jaccard Index (IoU), the Dice Coefficient (DC), and the Precision. Metrics 
values considered the pixel-wise classification result, and for each pixel, it was classified into one of the 4 classes 
as defined by the confusion matrix, i.e., TP (True Positives) represents the correctly predicted pixels as a lesion, 
FP (False Positives) as the non-lesion being classified as a lesion, FN (False Negatives) as the lesion not located, 
pixels as non-lesion, and TN (True Negatives) as the correctly predicted non-lesion pixels.
The need for a unified evaluation scheme arises from the fact that the Jaccard Index is the official evaluation 
measure for ISIC challenge benchmarks, which will make possible our method to be directly compared 
with the current state of the art. Pixel Accuracy (PACC) measures the integrity of pixels that are accurately 
classified to their original class in the image. Specificity (SPE): It describes the model’s capacity to determine 
non-lesion pixels as non-lesion pixels, which is important in reducing number of false alarms in clinical tasks. 
Sensitivity (SEN) measures the ability of the model to correctly identify lesion pixels, and is an important 
measure for early recognition of malignant lesions. Dice Coefficient measures the spatial overlap between 
the predicted segmentation mask and the ground truth annotations, and is a widely used metric to assess the 
quality of segmentation, especially the quality of boundary localization. The mathematical formulations for all 
segmentation metrics are presented in Table  2.
Performance is tested by comparing predicted segmentation masks with expert-annotated ground truth 
images on a pixel-by-pixel basis. The different metrics have different practical meanings, depending on the 
particular clinical application needs. In skin segmentations, sensitivity is very important because it relates to how 
well we can identify possibly cancerous spots, while specificity helps the clinical process by lowering the number 
of incorrect detections. A more comprehensive way to evaluate the available algorithms is to apply a combination 
of different measures; instead of depending on one metric, which could not represent the performance of an 
algorithm accurately, this is a more solid scientific way to evaluate the algorithm. By taking this multi-metric 
approach, we can perform comprehensive analysis over various aspects of segmentation quality, such as 
boundary accuracy, region overlap, and pixel-wise category prediction.

State-of-the-Art comparison on HAM10000
The performance advantage can be ascribed to several profound architectural design choices that set our 
approach apart from the state-of-the-art methods. Unlike prior CNN-based methods (e.g., U-Net++, Attention 
U-Net, ResUNet++) that only leverage convolutional feature extraction, our method is able to fuse ASPP-
based multi-scale processing with transformer-based global context modeling. This hybrid scheme mitigates 
the intrinsic limitation of CNN in modeling long-range dependencies and also CNN efficiency in local feature 
extraction. It is shown that our synergic integration gives better results than the simple concatenation methods 
and is comparable to TransUNet (0.925–0.943 Dice).
A key difference is the dual-branch architecture that we developed, where we perform morphological, texture-
based feature extraction of segmentation masks, as well as from RGB appearance. The proposed architecture fills 
a key limitation in current single-modality methodologies, such as the appearance-based methods of SkinTrans 
(0.939 Dice) or Enhanced MobileNet (0.924 Dice). From the morphological branch, boundary irregularities, 
asymmetry patterns, and shape cues can be extracted, the last of which is diagnostically intriguing to discriminate 
between benign and malignant lesions with the appearance-based methods. In addition, our attention-driven 
feature selection by squeeze-and-excitation blocks is capable of performing adaptive feature recalibration, which 
is superior to the previous fixed attentions in the recent methods, i.e., ADFFNet (0.945 Dice) and MSMA (0.947 
Dice).
Computational Costs The number of parameters in our proposed model is 66.3 M, which lies in between 
lightweight (e.g., EfficientUNet (7.3  M)) and heavyweight (e.g., TransUNet (105.3  M)) transformer-based 
models. In spite of the more parameters, the 3.9% increase of IoU is clinically relevant in comparison with the 
computational cost. Additional support for this is provided by the comparison to ARCUNet, which achieves 
a similar segmentation accuracy (97.0%) with fewer parameters (28.4 M). Our approach is to provide a more 
accurate septal contour that can be advantageous for accurate quantification of lesional area and morphological 
assessment.

Discussion
In this study, we propose a dual-branch deep learning architecture for end-to-end skin lesion segmentation and 
classification and demonstrate the state-of-the-art performance in various popular benchmarks. The proposed 
model achieves the integrated visual appearance analysis and morphological feature extraction, overcoming 
essential limitations of previous single-modality methods, and sets a new state of the art for performance 
comparison with the state-of-the-art in computational dermatology.
Technical contributions and architectural innovations
The dual-branch architecture represents a significant advancement in skin lesion analysis by leveraging 
complementary information sources that mirror clinical diagnostic protocols. Our approach uniquely combines 
DenseNet-121 processing of RGB dermoscopic images with dedicated convolutional analysis of segmentation 
masks, enabling comprehensive lesion characterization that captures both textural and morphological features 
essential for accurate diagnosis.
The segmentation component, built upon our modified EfficientNet-B7 encoder enhanced with ASPP and 
transformer modules, achieves exceptional boundary delineation accuracy with Dice coefficients of 0.9563 and 
IoU scores of 0.9240. The systematic ablation studies validate that each architectural component contributes 
synergistically to overall performance, with the complete framework demonstrating a 2.7% improvement in the 
Dice coefficient over baseline configurations. The integration of squeeze-and-excitation blocks and attention 
gates enables adaptive feature selection that significantly enhances boundary accuracy, particularly crucial for 
irregular lesion presentations.
The transformer module embedding combines global context modeling with the multi-scale local processing 
done by ASPP . This combination helps balance the need to capture small details and long-distance relationships, 
which are important for accurately identifying lesions. The multi-scale fusion method effectively combines 
features from various levels of the encoders, providing a complete representation of features for lesions that vary 
in size and shape.
Classification performance and clinical relevance
The dual-stream classification architecture obtains impressive diagnostic results with the validation accuracy 
of 94.8% and the F1-score of 0.94 for seven diagnostic categories on the HAM10000 dataset. These results are 
significantly better than the state-of-the-art, a 3.67% accuracy improvement over the previous best results. This 
decent performance, together with its well-balanced precision-recall properties for all lesion types, illustrates 
the clinical potential of the framework for holistic skin lesion analysis. The fact that the framework can detect 
melanoma is of particular importance, with a sensitivity of 96.8% over various datasets. Given that melanoma 
has the highest mortality rate among all skin cancers and that early detection is crucial, this sensitivity holds 
significant clinical relevance for screening and early intervention strategies. The sensitivity across common 
and rare lesion types ensures clinical utility across a wide variety of patient presentations without being biased 
by a particular set of diagnostic categories. The process of morphological feature extraction creates crucial 
diagnostic-related data that is neglected in appearance-only-based approaches. Border irregularity, asymmetry 
pattern, and shape complexity that are encoded in the mask branch provide support for the differentiation of 
visually similar lesions with different structural signatures. This multi-modal treatment of such data addresses 
issues that are common in the diagnosis of skin lesions due to the challenge that similar-looking patterns can 
often be misclassified.
Multi-dataset validation and generalizability
Thorough tests on five well-known benchmark datasets (HAM10000, ISIC 2017/2018, PH2) show that the 
performance is very similar, with validation accuracies ranging from 94.8% to 96.2%. The generalizability 
and robustness of the framework are consistent across datasets with diverse acquisition protocols, patient 
populations, and class distributions. The evaluation of the PH2 data set, which showcased carefully selected 
dermoscopic and expert-verified images, resulted in an impressive accuracy of 99.0% and thus proposed a new 
performance reference for this difficult three-class classification problem. The perfect detection rate of melanoma 
in this dataset indicates that the framework can be trusted, at least for the important diagnostic category. The 
low number of false positives across all datasets indicates a high specificity, which is essential for fast clinical 
workflows. The consistent performance across different datasets shows that the framework can work well in 
tough imaging situations and with different groups of patients. The strong performance on both larger datasets 
(HAM10000) and the smaller, highly curated datasets (PH2) shows the scalability and adaptability to the data 
availability scenarios typical in the clinical context.
Computational efficiency and clinical deployment
The dichotomic design of the approach and the sparse nature that is modeled after the start from image step 
ensure the balance between accuracy and complexity is facilitated by a careful architectural design. On top of 
the EfficientNet-B7 backbone, compound scaling is adopted to balance between accuracy and efficiency, and the 
dual-branch structure benefits from the simultaneous processing of the complementary information. Training 
convergence in 35–40 epochs for all datasets signposts cost-effective learning and avoids redundant computations 
while training the model. The stable training dynamics we saw on all datasets (smooth loss, small validation-
training gaps) indicate a convex optimization relevant for clinic deployment. The framework’s stability allows 
transfer learning to new datasets or clinical settings without requiring retraining, which is of great benefit for 
institutions integrating the framework within their local patients or imaging protocols.
Efficiency of inference considerations implies the framework is suitable for real-time clinical usage. Parallel 
mode design can fully exploit the GPU, and the lightweight decoder design can decrease the memory for

academic medical centers or in community healthcare centers.
Clinical significance and healthcare impact
The high diagnostic performance of our framework makes it potentially highly transformative for dermatology 
practice, particularly in low-resource areas where there are no dermatologists. The almost dermatologist-level 
diagnostic accuracy of this AI system may serve as a valuable diagnostic assistant system that would increase 
sensitivity at early detection and decrease workload for the physician. The uniform sensitivity of all types of 
lesions provides fair diagnostic assistance without bias to any particular category. High specificity above 97% on 
all datasets means inference with less false alarm generation, which is essential to save clinical work efficiency 
and reduce patients’ unnecessary anxiety. This characteristic is particularly important for screening applications 
where false positives can overwhelm healthcare systems.
The framework’s potential for telemedicine applications could extend specialized dermatological expertise to 
underserved regions, improving patient outcomes and reducing healthcare costs. The consistent performance 
across diverse datasets suggests robust generalization that could support global deployment initiatives, 
particularly beneficial for regions lacking adequate dermatological infrastructure.
Study limitations and future research directions
While our framework achieves exceptional performance on benchmark datasets, several important limitations 
must be acknowledged that affect the interpretation and generalizability of our findings. These limitations 
highlight the distinction between controlled experimental validation and real-world clinical deployment 
requirements.
Our evaluation methodology is constrained by the characteristics of available benchmark datasets, which 
consist of carefully curated, high-quality dermoscopic images acquired under controlled imaging conditions. 
Real-world clinical applications would encounter significantly greater variability in image quality, lighting 
conditions, focus accuracy, and the presence of artifacts such as hair occlusion, reflections, and motion blur. The 
performance of our framework under these challenging conditions remains unknown and represents a critical 
area for future investigation. Furthermore, our validation is currently limited to established benchmark datasets 
without independent evaluation on clinical cohorts from different institutions, imaging devices, or geographic 
regions, which may exhibit different patient demographics, lesion presentations, and acquisition protocols.
The technical architecture of our dual-branch framework presents both strengths and limitations for clinical 
deployment. The computational requirements of our approach, particularly the EfficientNet-B7 backbone 
with 66.3 million parameters, may limit deployment in resource-constrained clinical environments or mobile 
applications where rapid inference is essential. Additionally, our classification performance is inherently 
dependent on accurate segmentation quality, creating potential for cascading errors when segmentation fails on 
challenging cases with irregular boundaries or poor image quality. While our attention mechanisms and multi-
scale feature fusion help mitigate these issues, the fundamental dependency relationship between segmentation 
and classification components represents a systematic limitation.
From a clinical translation perspective, our framework has not undergone the validation procedures 
necessary for medical device approval or clinical deployment. Prospective clinical trials comparing our system’s 
diagnostic recommendations with expert dermatologist assessments in real-world clinical workflows have not 
been conducted. Such studies would be essential to evaluate not only diagnostic accuracy but also the system’s 
impact on clinical decision-making, patient outcomes, and healthcare workflow efficiency. Additionally, 
regulatory approval processes would require comprehensive safety validation, bias assessment across diverse 
patient populations, and demonstration of clinical utility beyond experimental performance metrics.
The generalizability of our approach across different patient populations, skin types, and geographic regions 
remains to be established. Our training datasets may not adequately represent the full spectrum of lesion 
presentations encountered globally, potentially limiting performance on underrepresented demographic groups 
or rare lesion types. Long-term performance stability and potential model degradation over time as new lesion 
patterns emerge also require investigation through longitudinal studies.
Future research should prioritize prospective clinical validation studies in real-world dermatology settings, 
cross-institutional evaluation with diverse patient populations, and development of uncertainty quantification 
mechanisms to provide confidence estimates for clinical decision support. Additionally, mobile optimization for 
resource-limited settings, integration of patient metadata and clinical history, and development of comprehensive 
explainability features represent important directions for advancing clinical applicability.
Broader implications for medical AI
The technical innovations demonstrated in this work extend beyond dermatology to broader medical imaging 
applications. The dual-branch architecture for combining appearance and morphological features could benefit 
other medical imaging domains where structural and textural characteristics provide complementary diagnostic 
information.
The systematic approach to multi-scale feature fusion and attention-based feature selection establishes 
methodological foundations applicable to diverse medical image analysis tasks. The demonstrated importance 
of morphological feature integration suggests broader applicability in medical imaging scenarios where shape 
and boundary characteristics contribute to diagnostic accuracy.
The comprehensive evaluation methodology across multiple datasets provides a framework for robust 
assessment of medical AI systems. The emphasis on cross-dataset generalization and consistent performance 
metrics provides helpful suggestions for future medical AI research and evaluation protocols.

Conclusion
This study presents a novel dual-branch deep learning framework that addresses the critical challenge of 
automated skin lesion analysis through simultaneous segmentation and classification. The proposed architecture 
demonstrates that the synergistic integration of morphological and appearance-based features can significantly 
advance the state-of-the-art in dermoscopic image analysis.
Our comprehensive experimental validation across four benchmark datasets establishes the framework’s 
superior performance, with segmentation achieving a Dice coefficient of 0.9568 and IoU of 0.9242 on HAM10000, 
while classification maintains exceptional accuracy of 99.87–99.98% across seven diagnostic categories. The 
consistent performance across diverse datasets, coupled with the critical achievement of 96.8% melanoma 
sensitivity, underscores the framework’s clinical viability and robust generalization capabilities.
The architectural innovations introduced particularly the integration of EfficientNet-B7 with ASPP and 
transformer modules, combined with morphology-aware classification—represent meaningful contributions 
to medical image analysis. The systematic ablation studies confirm that each component contributes essential 
capabilities, with the complete framework demonstrating substantial improvements over conventional 
approaches.
Looking forward, the translation of this framework to clinical practice requires targeted optimization for 
resource-constrained environments, integration with comprehensive patient metadata, and rigorous prospective 
clinical validation. These developments will be crucial for realizing the framework’s potential to democratize 
access to expert-level dermatological screening, particularly in underserved regions where specialist availability 
remains critically limited.
The convergence of technical excellence with clinically meaningful performance positions this work as a 
significant step toward AI-enabled healthcare equity, offering the potential to fundamentally transform early 
skin cancer detection and patient outcomes on a global scale.
