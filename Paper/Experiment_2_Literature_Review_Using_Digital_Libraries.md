# Experiment No. 2

## Title of Experiment
Literature Review using Digital Libraries

## Aim
To search, collect, review, and analyze research papers related to skin cancer detection using deep learning from major digital libraries such as Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore, SpringerLink, and ACM Digital Library, and to identify research gaps for project formulation.

## Learning Outcome
Search and review research papers using databases such as Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore, Springer, and ACM Digital Library.

## Introduction
Skin cancer is one of the most common cancers worldwide. Early diagnosis is very important because it improves treatment success and survival rate. Traditional diagnosis depends on visual inspection by dermatologists, which may be time-consuming and sometimes inaccurate. In recent years, deep learning methods, especially Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), transfer learning, and Vision Transformers, have shown promising results in skin lesion classification and melanoma detection. A literature review helps us understand the current state of research, identify limitations, and decide the direction of our project work.

## Objectives
- To understand the concept of literature review and its purpose in research.
- To search research papers using digital libraries.
- To identify relevant studies on skin cancer detection using deep learning.
- To study datasets, models, evaluation metrics, and findings.
- To identify research gaps and formulate a project problem.

## What is a Literature Review?
A literature review is a critical summary and analysis of existing research related to a specific topic. It helps the researcher understand what is already known, what methods have been used, which problems remain unsolved, and what new work is needed. It is an important step before designing a new project or experiment.

## Importance of Literature Review in Research
- Helps understand the problem deeply.
- Identifies the latest technologies and methods.
- Prevents repetition of existing work.
- Helps select appropriate datasets and algorithms.
- Helps identify gaps and form a research problem.
- Supports the design of methodology and experiments.

## What are Digital Libraries?
Digital libraries are online platforms that provide access to research papers, journals, conference proceedings, books, and articles. They help researchers find scholarly information quickly and reliably. In this study, major digital libraries were used to search and review papers.

## Major Digital Libraries

### 1. Google Scholar
- A free search engine for scholarly articles.
- Useful for locating papers across journals and publishers.
- Good for broad literature search.

### 2. IEEE Xplore
- Digital library of IEEE journals and conference papers.
- Strong for machine learning, image processing, and medical imaging studies.

### 3. SpringerLink
- Provides access to Springer journals and books.
- Useful for research in medical imaging and deep learning.

### 4. ACM Digital Library
- Gives access to ACM conference and journal papers.
- Helpful for research in AI, computer vision, and applied computing.

## Search Strategy
The literature search was done using a combination of keywords and Boolean operators. Search terms were entered in Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore, SpringerLink, and ACM Digital Library. The search focused on papers from 2020–2026, while some older foundational papers were included because they are important for understanding deep learning models.

## Keywords/Search Strings Used
- “skin cancer detection” AND “deep learning”
- “skin lesion classification” AND CNN
- “skin cancer classification” AND “transfer learning”
- “melanoma detection” AND “deep learning”
- “skin lesion” AND “convolutional neural network”
- “skin cancer” AND “EfficientNet”
- “skin lesion classification” AND ResNet
- “skin cancer” AND “Vision Transformer”

## Inclusion Criteria
- Peer-reviewed papers and conference proceedings.
- Papers related to skin cancer, melanoma, or skin lesion classification.
- Papers using deep learning, CNN, transfer learning, EfficientNet, ResNet, or Vision Transformer.
- Papers published between 2020 and 2026.
- Foundational papers older than 2020 if they are important for model understanding.

## Exclusion Criteria
- Papers not related to skin cancer or medical image analysis.
- Non-peer-reviewed blogs, web articles, or informal notes.
- Duplicate papers across libraries.
- Studies with incomplete information or unclear methodology.
- Repeated or low-quality conference papers without strong evidence.

## Literature Search Procedure
1. Selected the research topic: “Skin Cancer Detection Using Deep Learning”.
2. Used the digital libraries: Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore, SpringerLink, and ACM Digital Library.
3. Entered the required keywords and search strings.
4. Filtered papers by year, relevance, and peer-review status.
5. Read abstract, methodology, dataset, and results.
6. Shortlisted papers relevant to skin lesion classification and melanoma detection.
7. Compared their methods, outcomes, and limitations.
8. Documented the findings in a review table.

## Research Paper Selection Process
The search produced many papers. The relevant ones were shortlisted by checking:
- Title relevance
- Research objective
- Dataset used
- Method or model used
- Performance metrics
- Application to skin cancer detection
- Relevance to deep learning

After screening, 10 papers were selected for detailed review.

## Analysis of Existing Research
The reviewed literature shows that deep learning has become a major tool for skin cancer detection. Early papers such as Esteva et al. proved that CNNs can match dermatologist-level performance for skin lesion classification. Later, HAM10000 and ISIC datasets gave researchers large and diverse image sets, which improved model training. Review papers show that CNNs, transfer learning, and hybrid models are commonly used. Models such as ResNet, EfficientNet, and Vision Transformers are now widely applied because they improve feature extraction and classification. However, several challenges remain: class imbalance, limited dataset diversity, poor image quality, overfitting, weak generalization, and lack of explainability.

## Identification of Research Gaps
Common gaps identified across the literature are:
- Class imbalance: Malignant cases are often fewer than benign cases.
- Dataset limitations: Many datasets are small or have limited diversity.
- Image quality: Hair, lighting variation, blur, and noise reduce model accuracy.
- Overfitting: Models may perform well on training sets but fail on real clinical images.
- Lack of diverse datasets: Many datasets are biased toward lighter skin tones or limited lesion types.
- Model interpretability: Deep learning models often act like black boxes.
- Generalization to real-world clinical images: Models trained on benchmark datasets may not work well in actual hospitals.

These gaps are important because they affect the reliability and usefulness of automated diagnosis systems.

## How These Research Gaps Can Be Used to Formulate a Research Problem
These gaps can be converted into a strong research problem for a project. For example:

“Skin cancer detection using deep learning is a promising field, but existing models face challenges such as class imbalance, dataset scarcity, poor image quality, limited generalization, and lack of interpretability. Therefore, this study aims to develop a deep learning-based skin lesion classification model that improves melanoma detection accuracy using balanced preprocessing, transfer learning, and robust evaluation on real-world clinical images.”

This research problem is useful because it directly addresses real issues found in the literature and gives a clear project direction.

## Brief Explanation of Each Paper

### 1. Esteva et al. (2017)
This paper showed that CNN-based models can classify skin lesions at a level comparable to dermatologists. It used multiple skin image datasets and showed the value of deep learning in automated clinical diagnosis.

### 2. Tschandl et al. (2018)
This paper introduced HAM10000, a large dermatoscopic dataset. The main importance of this work is that it provided diverse lesion images needed for training deep learning models.

### 3. Codella et al. (2018)
This ISIC challenge paper focused on melanoma detection benchmarks. It helped standardize evaluation and encouraged research comparison across models.

### 4. Naqvi et al. (2023)
This review paper summarized deep learning approaches for skin cancer detection and showed that CNN-based methods are effective but still face dataset and interpretability issues.

### 5. Alwakid et al. (2022)
This paper used image enhancement and CNN classification for melanoma detection. It demonstrates how better preprocessing can improve accuracy.

### 6. Singh et al. (2025)
This IEEE paper presents a hybrid CNN with attention and learning-rate optimization that improves melanoma classification.

### 7. Brinker et al. (2019)
This study compared deep learning against dermatologists and showed that CNNs can perform better than many experts in melanoma diagnosis.

### 8. He et al. (2016)
This paper introduced ResNet, which solved deep network degradation and enabled better representation learning. It is foundational for medical image classification.

### 9. Tan and Le (2019)
This paper introduced EfficientNet, which is efficient and accurate. It is used in many medical imaging applications.

### 10. Dosovitskiy et al. (2021)
This paper introduced ViT, which showed that transformers can perform strongly in image recognition, including medical image tasks.

## Conclusion
The literature review shows that deep learning is a powerful technology for skin cancer detection and skin lesion classification. CNNs and transfer learning have significantly improved automated diagnosis, while newer models such as EfficientNet and Vision Transformers further enhance performance. However, important research gaps remain, especially in dataset diversity, class imbalance, image quality, model generalization, and interpretability. These gaps provide an excellent basis for identifying a meaningful project problem and designing a practical deep learning model for skin cancer detection. This literature review is therefore useful for both academic understanding and project development.

## Viva Questions with Answers

### Q1. What is a literature review?
A1. A literature review is a systematic study of existing research related to a topic. It helps understand previous work, identify gaps, and guide new research.

### Q2. Why are digital libraries important?
A2. Digital libraries provide access to research papers, journal articles, and conference proceedings from trusted sources. They help researchers find reliable and updated information.

### Q3. Why is Google Scholar useful in research?
A3. Google Scholar helps locate papers from different publishers and institutions quickly and is useful for reviewing the latest research.

### Q4. What is the difference between a review paper and a research paper?
A4. A review paper summarizes and compares existing studies, whereas a research paper presents new experiments, methods, and results.

### Q5. Why is transfer learning important in skin cancer detection?
A5. Transfer learning helps use pre-trained CNN models on large datasets, reducing training time and improving performance when medical datasets are small.

### Q6. What are the major challenges in skin cancer classification?
A6. Challenges include class imbalance, poor image quality, dataset limitations, lack of diverse samples, model overfitting, and poor generalization to real-world cases.

### Q7. Why is dataset diversity important?
A7. Different skin tones, lighting conditions, lesion shapes, and imaging devices affect performance. A diverse dataset makes the model more robust and realistic.

### Q8. How can literature gaps lead to a project topic?
A8. By identifying missing or weak areas in previous studies, the researcher can design a new problem statement and methodology that addresses those issues.
